Math Repo Review

The repository holds 722 manuscripts in 372 result families, written by an AI model, many of them claiming to settle famous open problems. This review separates what is machine-checked from what is not, re-runs the shipped checks, and adds independent tests of selected results.

Repository: mpast043/math (single commit adc7f12)Lean 4.34.1 · Mathlib d13f23bChecks run in a 4-core Linux container

Bottom line

  1. Treat every claim as an unrefereed preprint from a single source. The papers are dated 22 September to 6 October 2026, after my knowledge cutoff, so I cannot tell you whether experts have accepted any of them. The repository's own Lean catalogue lists its review status as unchecked.
  2. The Lean library is the strongest evidence. It has 121,734 files and 25.9 million lines, with no sorry, no custom axioms and no native_decide. There are 405 Comparator challenges, and 197 of the 372 families have a formal statement that matches the headline claim. Another 38 formalize only part of the claim.
  3. I compiled and kernel-checked two headline proofs end to end. Seymour's second-neighbourhood conjecture and the non-amenability of Thompson's group F both build from source, match their challenge statements declaration by declaration, and use only the three standard axioms. The 14 headline Lean statements I audited state the real conjectures faithfully.
  4. The shipped computational certificates hold up. All 14 bundles I re-ran pass. One bundle is missing a manifest file its runner needs.
  5. The overview sometimes claims more than Lean proves. The Hadwiger counterexample, the uniform bound in Hilbert's sixteenth problem, "exactly three" MUBs in dimension six and 2D Anderson localization are not formalized. The Lean statements cover weaker or supporting results.
  6. 137 families have no formal proof. These include full BSD in low rank, Hilbert's tenth problem over ℚ, the Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties, L = RL = BPL, 20.49n Subset Sum and the Haldane gap. They need ordinary expert refereeing before anyone builds on them.

How to read the evidence

Each family gets one evidence level. The levels describe what exists and what I checked. None of them replaces expert review of the mathematics.

Kernel-checked here

I built the proof with Lean 4.34.1 and the pinned Mathlib, restated the challenge independently, derived it from the compiled proof and confirmed it uses only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

Lean: headline

The repository has a sorry-free Lean proof, and its Comparator statement matches the headline claim according to the repository's scope notes and my reading. I did not compile these; most need all of Mathlib.

Lean: partial

A Lean proof exists, but its statement is weaker than the headline, or covers one supporting step. The note on each row says what is missing.

Paper only

No formal proof. Some of these ship computational certificates for their finite steps; the analytic arguments are unchecked.

A Comparator challenge is a Lean file that states a theorem with sorry. The Comparator tool checks that a compiled proof proves exactly that statement with only the three standard axioms. The statement file is the trust anchor: if its definitions are wrong, the proof is worthless. That is why I audited statements as well as proofs.

What I verified myself

Four kinds of checks, each independent of the repository's own claims of success.

Lean kernel check: Seymour's conjecture

Family 173. Every finite oriented graph has a vertex with at least as many vertices at distance two as at distance one. Open since 1990.

How the proof works: a smallest counterexample satisfies a strict subset-deficit inequality. Replacing each vertex by a large transitive tournament sharpens it, and an extremal argument on compatible families of ordered pairs, with a pruning lemma, contradicts it. The paper is about 1,100 lines of TeX.

  • Built 43 proof files (4.9K lines) with about 1,800 Mathlib and dependency modules from source: 1,878 jobs, 24 min 45 s, no errors.
  • Copied the challenge statement verbatim into a fresh namespace and derived it from the compiled theorem.
  • Both depend only on [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound].
  • leanchecker --fresh replayed every declaration, Mathlib included: passed in 4 min 22 s.
  • Comparator-style comparison of the compiled challenge and proof: all 11 declarations identical. A deliberately mutated statement is flagged.
theorem exists_goodVertex (r : V → V → Prop) (hr : IsOriented r) :
    ∃ v, GoodVertex r v

Lean kernel check: Thompson's group F

Family 248. Thompson's group F of dyadic piecewise-linear homeomorphisms of [0, 1] is not amenable. Open since the 1970s, after several disputed or withdrawn claims in both directions.

How the proof works: amenability would give Følner sets. Averaging recursively defined Hilbert-space colours of dyadic partitions over such a set produces an approximate fixed point of a Lipschitz self-map of the Hilbert ball, which a Benyamini–Sternfeld map with displacement at least 1/2 rules out. The paper is about 800 lines of TeX.

  • Built 68 proof files (9.5K lines) with the pinned Mathlib from source: 3,083 jobs, 41 min, no errors.
  • Comparator-style comparison: all 61 declarations of the challenge file, including its own definitions of F, PL witnesses and invariant means, are identical in the compiled proof.
  • The theorem depends only on [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound].
  • leanchecker --fresh replayed every declaration, Mathlib included: passed in 7 min 58 s.
theorem thompson_F_nonamenable_composition :
    ∃ group : Group F, letI := group;
      (∀ h g x, (h * g).val x = h.val (g.val x)) ∧
      ¬ Nonempty (InvariantMean F)

Statement audit

I read the Comparator statements for 14 headline results and checked them against the standard formulations.

  • Faithful: quasi-RH (Mathlib's riemannZeta), Catalan's constant, μ(π) = 2, Unique Games (polynomial-time Turing-machine reduction), ω ≤ 9/4 (straight-line programs over ℂ), Seymour, Thompson's F, circulant Hadamard, both Kaplansky counterexamples, Cannon, Kadison similarity.
  • Weaker than the headline: MUBs in dimension six (Lean proves at most 5, the paper claims 3) and 2D Anderson localization (Lean proves only the almost-sure spectrum).
  • Scanned all 25.9M lines: zero sorry, zero axiom declarations, zero native_decide or implemented_by.

Shipped certificates re-run

14 bundles, each copied to a scratch directory and run with its documented command.

  • All pass: Mahler, triangular lattice, two matrix-multiplication certificates, Bernoulli convolutions, Snaky, cycle–clique Ramsey, Saxl (61 degrees), Foulkes, Hadamard order 6 (output matches the paper's appendix), Campana–Peternell, zero-key entanglement, Einstein 4-manifolds, harmonic dimension.
  • Defect: the Mahler runner requires SOURCE-MANIFEST.json, which is not in the repository. The ten programs it pins pass when run directly.

Independent tests

New code, written for this review, that could have exposed a false claim.

  • Seymour (173): all 14,348,907 oriented graphs on 6 vertices and 4 million random ones up to 40 vertices; no counterexample.
  • Cycle–clique Ramsey (189): SAT solver confirms R(Cm, Kn) = (m−1)(n−1)+1 for (4,3), (5,3), (6,3), (7,3) and (4,4).
  • Amplitude damping (276): the capacity formula matches King's theorem to 1.7·10−16 and brute-force optimization to 10−11; entangled two-use inputs never beat it.
  • Entropy photon-number inequality (273): 652 random non-Gaussian inputs, smallest margin +3.3·10−3; no violation.
  • Quintic Liénard (143): both limit cycles of the paper's example found at amplitudes 1.503 and 4.200. A random scan of other quintics did not finish within its time limit.

Where the overview claims more than Lean proves

From the repository's own scope notes for all 235 formalized families. These are the gaps with the largest consequences; the catalogue's "Lean: partial" filter lists all 38.

  • 157 Hadwiger's conjecture. Only the bound χlist ≤ C·h(G) is formal. The counterexamples to Hadwiger and Colin de Verdière are not.
  • 143 Hilbert's sixteenth problem. Only "at most two limit cycles for quintic Liénard systems" is formal. The uniform bound for all polynomial fields is not.
  • 266 MUBs in dimension six. Lean proves at most 5. "Exactly 3" rests on a floating-point computation outside Lean.
  • 261 Anderson model. Lean identifies the spectrum [−4−h, 4+h]. Pure-point spectrum at every disorder in 2D is not formal.
  • 267 Bose–Einstein condensation. Zero-temperature condensation of the dilute hard-sphere gas is formal; the positive-temperature claim is not.
  • 260 Penrose inequality, 263 ionization. Only supporting constructions and asymptotics are formal; the inequality and the Z + CM binding bound are not.
  • 194 Lech's conjecture, 240 Shelah's categoricity, 312 homotopy hypothesis. Only a supporting step of each is formal.
  • 150 Triangle billiards. Ergodicity for every irrational triangle is formal; weak mixing is not.
  • 197 Kaplansky direct finiteness. The formal counterexample uses a group with torsion. Torsion-freeness, and the non-soficity that follows from Elek–Szabó, are not formalized.
  • 213, 215 Percolation and O(n) models. Formal for ℤ³ and for exponential decay in 2D O(n), both famous problems in their own right; the more general claims are not.

What these results could be used for

If the proofs hold, these are the parts that other fields could build on. Results marked Lean have a formal proof in the repository; the rest are paper-only.

Quantum information and communication

  • Thermal qubit channels (276, Lean). The classical capacity of generalized amplitude damping is a one-variable maximization, and entangled coding gives no gain. This applies directly to qubit memories and links that relax toward a nonzero thermal population. The chart below is computed from it.
  • Optical channels (273, Lean). The entropy photon-number inequality is the missing step for capacity regions of the bosonic broadcast and wiretap channels proposed by Guha, Shapiro and Erkmen. The paper states those consequences, but they are outside the Lean statement.
  • Limits of shallow quantum circuits (274, Lean). Constant-depth QAC0 circuits cannot compute parity, which constrains low-depth quantum advantage arguments.
  • Device-independent protocols (277, Lean). Threshold parallel repetition for entangled games gives exponential concentration, which certification and randomness-expansion protocols rely on.

Algorithms and complexity

  • Approximation limits (102, 125, Lean). The Unique Games theorem makes 0.878 optimal for Max-Cut and 2 optimal for Vertex Cover unless P = NP. The value 1 + 2/e is the exact threshold for metric k-median.
  • Matrix multiplication (107, Lean). ω ≤ 9/4 lowers the exponent of every algorithm that reduces to matrix multiplication. The constants are astronomically large, so the gain is theoretical.
  • Counting and sampling (113, 115, 131, Lean). These give polynomial-time approximate counts of perfect matchings (hafnians of 0–1 matrices), exact uniform sampling of contingency tables with fixed margins (exact conditional tests in statistics), and polynomial mixing of the edge-switch chain for random graphs with given degrees.
  • Strings and sequences (121, 128, 122, Lean). These give a near-linear-time (1+ε) edit-distance approximation, a 2-approximation for shortest common superstring (the combinatorial core of sequence assembly), and trace-reconstruction bounds relevant to DNA data storage.
  • Paper only: L = RL = BPL (103), Subset Sum in 20.49n (138), integer multiplication below n log n (109), deterministic polynomial factoring over 𝔽p (142).

Condensed matter and statistical physics

  • Magnetism (271, Lean). Spontaneous magnetization of the quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet in d ≥ 3, for every spin, was a long-standing gap behind textbook pictures of ferromagnetic order.
  • Fractional quantum Hall effect (269, Lean). The Laughlin state at filling 1/3 is uniformly gapped for the V1 interaction.
  • Mass gap and criticality (215, 213, partial). The formal parts give exponential decay for the 2D O(n) models with n ≥ 3 at all temperatures (Polyakov's prediction) and no percolation at criticality on ℤ³.
  • Disordered systems (221, 234, 235, Lean; 222, partial). These cover the Mézard–Parisi formula for diluted spin glasses, the existence and computability of random k-SAT thresholds, and perceptron free energies (the capacity of simple neural networks; only the spherical model is formal).
  • Paper only: the spin-one Haldane gap (268), the 2D area law with polynomial-bond-dimension PEPS (265), which would justify tensor-network simulation of gapped 2D systems, and BKT scaling for the XY model (216).

Fluids, plasmas, chemistry and biology

  • Plasma physics (362, Lean). The 3D relativistic Vlasov–Maxwell system has global smooth solutions for large data, without smallness or symmetry assumptions.
  • Reaction networks (149, Lean). Every weakly reversible mass-action network is permanent: concentrations stay between positive bounds for all rate constants. This is a robustness guarantee for models in systems biology and chemical engineering.
  • Fluid computation (376, Lean). Smooth forced Navier–Stokes flows can simulate any Turing machine, so some questions about particle trajectories in forced viscous flow are undecidable.
  • Electronic structure (275, 373, Lean; 278, paper). QMA-hardness of the continuum Coulomb ground-state energy is a complexity barrier for exact quantum chemistry. A three-marginal Coulomb Monge problem with no optimal map affects strictly correlated electron DFT. A failure of Kohn–Sham ensemble representability (paper only) concerns the foundations of DFT.

Number theory and cryptography

  • Zero-free half-plane (003, Lean). A zero-free region Re s > 7/8 for every Dirichlet L-function gives prime-counting and progression errors O(x7/8+ε) uniformly. Excluding Siegel zeros makes effective several classical bounds, such as class-number lower bounds, that were known only ineffectively. The README says the companion 11/12 write-up was human-edited.
  • Diophantine approximation (005, 017, Lean). Catalan's constant is irrational and the irrationality exponent of π is 2. These are statements about specific constants.
  • Paper only: full BSD in Selmer corank at most one (002), Goldfeld's conjecture (006), modularity over imaginary quadratic fields (030), Artin's primitive roots for every base (029).

Recommended next steps

  1. Run Comparator on all 405 challenges in CI with a Mathlib cache. My Seymour check reproduces its logic by hand. A full run settles whether every listed proof compiles against its exact statement.
  2. Have domain experts audit the challenge statements, especially those with custom definitions, such as Thompson's F, the MUB model, the Unique Games encoding and the matrix-multiplication cost model. A proof of a mis-stated theorem proves nothing.
  3. Referee the 137 paper-only families, starting with the claims that would matter most: BSD in low rank, Hilbert's tenth problem over ℚ, Hodge for CM abelian varieties, L = RL = BPL, Subset Sum, Kakeya in three dimensions, the Haldane gap and strong cosmic censorship.
  4. Re-verify the floating-point exclusion behind "exactly three MUBs in dimension six" with interval arithmetic or in Lean.
  5. Fix the missing SOURCE-MANIFEST.json in the Mahler verification bundle.
