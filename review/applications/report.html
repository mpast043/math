Quantum information

Family 276 · quantum memories and links

How many classical bits can a decaying qubit hold?

Lean: headlinePaper (PDF)
Open question
A superconducting qubit left idle relaxes toward a thermal state. That process is the generalized amplitude-damping channel. Its classical capacity, the most information the qubit can carry per use with the best encoding and decoding, was known only at zero temperature or in special cases.
What the catalogue proves
The capacity is reached by two pure signal states √(1−p)|0⟩ ± √p|1⟩ with collective decoding, so it is a one-variable maximization. It is additive: entangling inputs across uses, or with any other channel, does not raise it.
How to use it
Set the damping γ = 1 − e−t/T₁ and the thermal population ν = 1/(1 + ehf/kT), then maximize over p:
C = maxp [ h((1−γ)p + γν) − h(½ + ½√(1−4u)) ]
u = γν(1−ν) + γ(1−γ)(p−ν)², h = binary entropy in bits
What I computed
For a 5 GHz qubit with T₁ = 100 µs, after waiting one T₁ at 50 mK the capacity is 0.361 bit. Writing the bit as |0⟩ or |1⟩ and reading in that basis gives 0.206 bit, so the optimal code carries 75% more. The best signal states put about 41% weight on |1⟩. Capacity halves after 62 µs at 30–50 mK and after 57 µs at 100 mK.
Limits
Capacity is an asymptotic rate. It needs long codes and joint measurements across many stored qubits; one-shot readout gets less.
Family 273 · optical communication

What rates can one optical transmitter send to a near and a far receiver?

Lean: headlinePaper (PDF)
Open question
A beam splitter, a fibre tap or a diverging free-space beam delivers a fraction ηB of the light to a near receiver and ηC to a far one. In 2007 Guha, Shapiro and Erkmen found a coding scheme for this broadcast channel and showed it is optimal if the entropy photon-number inequality (EPnI) holds. The inequality stayed unproved, so the capacity region was unknown.
What the catalogue proves
The EPnI for any number of modes, with entanglement allowed inside each input. This completes the converse: the capacity region is the one below, reached by superposition coding with coherent states. A corollary gives the minimum output entropy of a thermal-noise attenuator.
How to use it
With N mean photons per use, sweep 0 ≤ β ≤ 1:
RB ≤ g(ηB β N), RC ≤ g(ηC N) − g(ηC β N)
g(x) = (x+1) log₂(x+1) − x log₂ x
What I computed
When the near receiver gets half its maximum rate, superposition coding gives the far receiver 6% more than time sharing for a 50/25 fibre split at N = 1, 11% at N = 10, and 28–31% more for a free-space link with 60% and 10% collection at N = 10–100.
Limits
Pure-loss channels only (no added thermal noise), and the rates assume ideal joint-detection receivers.

Family 261 · disordered materials

Does any amount of disorder make a two-dimensional material insulating?

Lean: partial2D paper (PDF)3D paper (PDF)
Open question
The scaling theory of localization (Abrahams, Anderson, Licciardello and Ramakrishnan, 1979) predicts that in two dimensions every electron state is localized at any disorder, while three dimensions keep a metallic phase when disorder is weak. Neither statement had a proof for the Anderson model, and the 2D one cannot be settled by simulation: the localization length grows so fast as disorder decreases that it outruns any sample.
What the catalogue proves
For hopping 1 and site energies uniform on [−W/2, W/2]: in 2D the spectrum is pure point (all states localized) for every W > 0; in 3D and higher, weak disorder leaves an energy interval of absolutely continuous spectrum (extended states).
How to use it
Treat weakly disordered 2D systems (thin films, 2D photonic or cold-atom lattices with on-site disorder) as insulators in the infinite-size limit, however metallic a finite sample looks. In 3D, the metallic phase at weak disorder is now a theorem rather than an extrapolation.
What I computed
The level-spacing ratio ⟨r⟩ near the band centre is 0.531 for extended states and 0.386 for localized ones. In 2D, ⟨r⟩ falls with system size for every W ≥ 6 (W = 8: 0.504, 0.478, 0.441 for L = 16, 32, 64). For W ≤ 4 the 64 × 64 samples stay at the extended value (W = 4: 0.533): their localization length is longer than the sample, so simulation alone would call them metals. The theorem says they are insulators. In 3D, ⟨r⟩ stays near 0.53 at every size for W ≤ 14, sits near the critical value at W = 16.5 (0.513), and falls with size for W ≥ 19: the metal–insulator transition at the known Wc ≈ 16.5, whose metallic side the 3D theorem establishes.
Limits
The audit found that Lean covers only the almost-sure spectrum. The 2D localization theorem is paper-only and is the claim to referee first.
Family 268 · quantum magnets

Is the spin-1 Heisenberg chain gapped?

Paper onlyPeriodic gap (PDF)Boundary-field gap (PDF)
Open question
Haldane predicted in 1983 that antiferromagnetic chains of integer spin have an energy gap while half-integer chains do not. Experiments on nickel chain compounds (NENP, Y₂BaNiO₅) and numerics (DMRG: 0.41048 J) agree, and the gapped Haldane phase became the standard example of a symmetry-protected topological phase. A proof existed only for the AKLT model, not for the Heisenberg chain itself.
What the catalogue proves
On even periodic rings, the gap of H = Σ Si·Si+1 stays above a positive constant as the ring grows. A companion proves a gap for odd open chains with an end field and builds boundary states with topological index −1.
How to use it
Spin-1 chain models of real compounds can rely on a bulk gap, so correlations decay exponentially and the ground state is short-range entangled. The value of the gap still comes from numerics.
What I computed
On periodic rings the gap falls from 1.000 J at L = 4 to 0.4428 J at L = 16, and a fit Δ + a e−L/ξ to L ≥ 8 gives Δ = 0.419 J, against the DMRG value 0.4105 J. On an open chain of 16 sites the lowest triplet lies only 0.0496 J above the singlet, a splitting that shrinks like e−L/5.7 (the spin correlation length is about 6), while the next level stays 0.60 J up. In that triplet the magnetization sits at the ends: +0.52 on each end site, alternating and decaying inward, and at most 0.086 on the four central sites. A bulk excitation would peak in the middle instead. This is a free spin-½ at each end, the signature of the Haldane phase.
Limits
The proof gives positivity, not 0.41 J. Neither paper has a Lean formalization.
Family 267 · ultracold atoms

How much of a Bose–Einstein condensate is not condensed?

Lean: partialDepletion paper (PDF)Positive temperature (PDF)
Open question
Bogoliubov's 1947 theory predicts that interactions push a fraction (8/3√π)√(ρa³) of atoms out of the condensate even at zero temperature, with a definite momentum distribution. Whether an interacting gas in three dimensions condenses at all at positive temperature was also unproved.
What the catalogue proves
For hard spheres, with the thermodynamic limit taken first: the full Bogoliubov momentum distribution of the depleted atoms, hence the depletion law, and the depletion law also for bounded repulsive potentials. A companion proves condensation at a positive temperature for the dilute hard-sphere gas.
How to use it
Compute ρa³ from density and scattering length. The depleted fraction is 1.5045√(ρa³). The depleted atoms have momentum profile g(k/kξ) with kξ = √(8πρa), which falls off as (4πρa)²/k⁴, the Bogoliubov value of Tan's contact.
What I computed
Typical sodium and rubidium condensates lose 0.2–0.6% of atoms to depletion. Feshbach-enhanced potassium in a box trap at a = 3000 a₀ reaches 6.7%, large enough to measure. The total mass of the Bogoliubov profile integrates to 1.504506, matching 8/(3√π).
Limits
Leading order in √(ρa³). Near ρa³ ~ 10⁻³ higher-order corrections matter, and trapped clouds need a local-density average.
Family 262 · stability of matter

How much kinetic energy must a gas of electrons carry?

Lean: headlinePaper (PDF)
Open question
The Lieb–Thirring inequality bounds the kinetic energy of N fermions by their density, T ≥ K ∫ρ5/3. It is the analytic core of the proof that ordinary matter is stable. The best constant K is a 50-year-old problem; the conjecture is that K equals the Thomas–Fermi value. Even the one-dimensional version was open for 1/2 < γ < 3/2.
What the catalogue proves
In one dimension, for 1/2 < γ < 3/2 and matrix-valued potentials of any size, the sharp constant is the scalar one-bound-state value. Equality holds only for sech² solitons.
How to use it
Matrix-valued bounds in one dimension lift to any dimension by the Laptev–Weidl argument. The ratio to the classical constant is Rγ = 2((2γ−1)/(2γ+1))γ−1/2; lifting at γ = 1 gives L1,3 ≤ (2/√3)·Lcl1,3, and the kinetic constant follows by duality.
What I computed
The kinetic-energy constant improves to K ≥ 8.28, which is 90.9% of the Thomas–Fermi value 9.12 (one spin state, ħ²/2m = 1). The 2021 bound of Frank, Hundertmark, Jex and Nam gives 77.8%. Numerically, sech² wells saturate the 1D bound, and 18 random 2×2 and 3×3 matrix potentials stay below it (largest ratio 0.998).
Limits
This is an upper bound on the 3D Lieb–Thirring constant. The 3D conjecture K = KTF remains open.
Family 090 · two-dimensional crystals

Which crystal do screened charges form in two dimensions?

Lean: headlinePaper (PDF)
Open question
Dusty plasmas, charged colloids at interfaces and electrons on liquid helium crystallize under screened Coulomb (Yukawa) repulsion e−κr/r. Simulations always find the triangular lattice, but no proof covered all configurations, periodic or not, at every screening length.
What the catalogue proves
Universal optimality in the plane (the Cohn–Kumar conjecture): the triangular lattice minimizes energy per particle for every completely monotone function of squared distance, among all configurations of the same density. The Yukawa potential is such a function for every κ.
How to use it
Take the triangular lattice as the exact zero-temperature ground state, and its lattice sum as the reference energy, for melting-curve and defect calculations at any κ.
What I computed
At density 1, the square lattice costs +0.22% at κ = 0.5 and +7.6% at κ = 4. Honeycomb and kagome cost 1.4–44%, and randomly displacing the triangular lattice always raised the energy.
Limits
Ground states only. The theorem says nothing about melting, and nothing about potentials with attraction, which are not completely monotone.

Dynamics, chemistry and biology

Family 146 · Hamiltonian chaos

Is the chaos in the standard map real?

Lean: headlinePaper (PDF)
Open question
The Chirikov standard map, p′ = p + K sin x, x′ = x + p′, is the textbook model of Hamiltonian chaos: the kicked rotor, particles in circular accelerators, magnetic field lines in tokamaks. Simulations show chaos on almost all of phase space for large K, but stable islands persist for arbitrarily large K, and Sinai's conjecture that the metric entropy is positive stayed open.
What the catalogue proves
The map has positive metric entropy with respect to area for every sufficiently large K. By Pesin's formula, chaotic orbits fill a set of positive area.
How to use it
Models that reduce to the standard map now have provably chaotic dynamics at strong kicking. The entropy, close to ln(K/2) per kick, sets how fast predictions lose accuracy: a small error grows by a factor of about K/2 per kick, tenfold at K = 20.
What I computed
From 25,600 orbits of 2,000 iterations, the area-averaged Lyapunov exponent is within 0.011 of ln(K/2) for every K ≥ 10 (3.912 against 3.912 at K = 100). Chaotic orbits cover 100% of the torus except near the accelerator modes at K = 2π and 4π, where islands keep 0.25% and 0.07% of the area.
Limits
The theorem gives positivity for large K, not the value ln(K/2) and not a threshold value of K.
Family 149 · chemical reaction networks

Can a reaction network lose a species, whatever its rate constants?

Lean: headlinePaper (PDF)
Open question
Biochemical and ecological models usually come with a known reaction graph and unknown rates. The permanence conjecture says that if every reaction lies on a directed cycle of complexes (weak reversibility), then for any rates no species dies out and none blows up. It was known for complex-balanced systems, in low dimension and for one linkage class.
What the catalogue proves
Every weakly reversible mass-action system, with any positive rate constants and any deficiency, is permanent: each positive stoichiometric class has a compact absorbing set inside the positive orthant.
How to use it
Check one graph property by hand: does every reaction y → y′ have a path of reactions back from y′ to y? If so, every concentration eventually stays between positive bounds, for every choice of rates.
What I computed
300 random weakly reversible networks (4 species, two or three linkage classes, deficiency 1–5, rates spread over two decades), each started three times with some species at 10−6 of a typical level: all 900 runs returned to the same long-run state as a start in the interior (ratio of long-run minima 0.988–1.000) and none blew up. Removing one reaction so that weak reversibility fails led to extinction in 123 runs and blow-up in 60 (20%).
Limits
Mass-action kinetics with constant rates. The theorem bounds the long-run state, not the time it takes to recover.

Family 131 · network science

Can random graphs with given degrees be sampled correctly for every network?

Lean: headlinePaper (PDF)
Open question
To decide whether a network has more triangles, motifs or assortativity than chance, one compares it with uniformly random graphs that have the same degrees. The edge-switch Markov chain is the standard sampler, but its mixing was proved only for restricted degree sequences (the Kannan–Tetali–Vempala conjecture).
What the catalogue proves
The lazy switch chain mixes in O(n⁸) steps for every graphical degree sequence, and degree-constrained graphs can be sampled exactly uniformly in expected polynomial time.
How to use it
Use the switch chain for any degree sequence, however heterogeneous, and measure its autocorrelation time to space the samples. The polynomial bound is a correctness guarantee; real mixing is far faster.
What I computed
On all 19,355 cubic graphs on 8 labelled vertices, the exact transition matrix is symmetric, so the stationary law is uniform, and it mixes in 38 steps (bound: 3.4 × 10⁷). On Zachary's karate club (34 nodes, 78 edges, 45 triangles), rejection sampling produced 0 simple graphs in 20,000 tries, and the "erased" configuration model drops 12 of 78 edges and predicts 17.5 ± 4.7 triangles, which would wrongly make 45 look extreme (z ≈ 5.9). The switch chain gives 39.3 ± 4.5, z = 1.3: the club's triangles are mostly explained by its degrees.
Limits
The O(n⁸) bound is far too large to set run lengths; use measured autocorrelation (here about one switch attempt per edge).
Families 011 and 012 · cryptography

How often are p − 1, or two neighbouring integers, smooth?

011: Paper only012: Lean: headlinep − 1 paper (PDF)n, n + 1 paper (PDF)
Open question
Pollard's p − 1 method factors an RSA modulus when p − 1 has only small prime factors, and isogeny-based signatures need pairs of consecutive smooth integers. Both rely on the heuristic that p − 1, and the pair n, n + 1, behave like independent random integers. The Ford–Konyagin–Luca and Erdős–Pomerance conjectures made that heuristic precise.
What the catalogue proves
The prime factors of p − 1 follow the Poisson–Dirichlet law, so P(p − 1 is p1/u-smooth) → ρ(u), Dickman's function. The largest prime factors of n and n + 1 are asymptotically independent, so P(both smooth) → ρ(u)².
How to use it
Stage 1 of Pollard p − 1 with bound B succeeds on a random prime p with probability ρ(u), u = log p / log B. A first-order correction ρ(u − κ/log B), with κ = Σ log q/(q−1)² = 1.2270, accounts for the extra small primes that always divide p − 1. Searches for twin smooth integers find pairs at density ρ(u)².
What I computed
Up to 10⁸, p − 1 is smoother than a random integer at every size, but the excess shrinks with each decade and the κ-correction predicts it (u = 1.5: measured 1.126, predicted 1.121). A random 512-bit prime falls to stage 1 with B = 2⁴⁰ with probability 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁶, so the old "strong prime" rules buy almost nothing. Near 2²⁵⁶, pairs that are both 2³²-smooth occur at density 2−49.8, so about 2²⁰⁶ such pairs exist in [X, 2X].
Limits
These are limit laws. ρ(u) is the leading term at cryptographic sizes, and for very small B the true twin-smooth density is larger than ρ(u)².
Family 374 · optimal transport in machine learning

How much can an optimal transport map move when the target is slightly wrong?

Lean: headlinePaper (PDF)
Open question
Transport maps are estimated from data in domain adaptation, generative modelling and single-cell trajectory inference. The error in the map is controlled by a stability inequality ‖Tμ − Tν‖ ≤ C·W₂(μ, ν)α. Letrouit conjectured α = 1/2 for a uniform source.
What the catalogue proves
α = 1/3 holds uniformly over targets in a compact set, and 1/3 is sharp even for three-atom targets, which disproves the square-root conjecture.
How to use it
Budget map error as W₂1/3 in the worst case. The bad case is a target with a small-mass cluster: moving it costs almost nothing in W₂, but its transport cell tilts and reassigns a much larger region of the source.
What I computed
With my own semi-discrete solver (exact power diagrams, Newton on the dual weights), the paper's family reproduces its closed forms to six digits and gives slope 0.3333. Equal-mass targets give 0.5003. For a fixed central mass m the slope is 1/2, with ‖ΔT‖ = 0.7071·m−1/4·W₂1/2 at both m = 1/3 and m = 0.01. The constant grows as m shrinks, and choosing m to match the perturbation gives the exponent 1/3.
Limits
Uniform source on a convex body and targets in a fixed compact set. Smoother targets with mass bounded below behave like the square-root case.

Reproduce

Every number on this page comes from a script in review/applications/, run with Python 3.13, NumPy, SciPy and NetworkX in a 4-core container with 16 GB of memory. Each script writes a text report and a JSON file to results/. Run them from that directory.

cd review/applications
python3 qubit_memory_capacity.py         # family 276, seconds
python3 optical_broadcast.py             # family 273, seconds
python3 anderson_level_statistics.py     # family 261, about 10 minutes
python3 haldane_gap.py results 16        # family 268, about 7 minutes, several GB
python3 bose_gas_depletion.py            # family 267
python3 lieb_thirring.py                 # family 262
python3 screened_coulomb_crystals.py     # family 090
python3 standard_map_entropy.py          # family 146, about 1 minute
python3 reaction_network_permanence.py results 300   # family 149, a few minutes
python3 switch_chain_null_models.py      # family 131
python3 smooth_numbers_crypto.py results 1e8         # families 011 and 012, several GB
python3 ot_stability.py                  # family 374, under a second
  • Dickman's ρ is computed from its power series on each unit interval in 60-digit decimal arithmetic; 40- and 90-digit runs agree to 10 significant figures up to u = 20 (ρ(10) = 2.770172 × 10⁻¹¹, ρ(20) = 2.461783 × 10⁻²⁹).
  • The transport solver does not use the paper's potentials; it finds the dual weights by Newton's method on exact polygon areas.
  • Simulations can contradict a theorem but cannot confirm one. Where a demonstration matches a theorem, that is consistency, not proof.
